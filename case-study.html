The problem

Journaling apps had become too much.

Most journaling apps in 2024 felt like productivity software. Streaks, scores, AI summaries, mood graphs. Nothing about that feels like sitting down with a notebook and a cup of tea.

I wanted to build the opposite: an app that gets out of your way. One that respects silence. One that doesn't ping you.

Research

12 interviews.
One clear signal.

"I deleted Day One because it made journaling feel like a job. I just wanted a clean page."
— P04, designer, 28
11/12
wanted fewer features
Participants explicitly said current apps had too many tabs, modes, and prompts.
8/12
disabled streaks
Most found gamification stressful — it turned a soft habit into a hard one.
12/12
missed paper
Every single participant compared their ideal digital experience to a physical notebook.
The design

One screen. One page.
That's it.

I stripped everything out. No tabs. No moods. No streaks. The app opens to today's empty page. You write. You close it. That's the loop.

Entries from previous days live behind a subtle swipe, like flipping back through a physical journal. Search is one-handed, fast, fuzzy.
